# Category Design Playbooks

Use this reference only after the product category has been identified from product facts, Amazon category, competitor materials, or user-provided context.

These playbooks provide category-specific image strategy. They must not override product truth, approved claims, local detail locks, Amazon policy, or the user's requested deliverables.

## Category Match Gate

Before applying any playbook, record:

- `Detected category`: normalized category name.
- `Evidence`: product name, Amazon category, competitor set, use case, and visible product form.
- `Match confidence`: `high`, `medium`, or `low`.
- `Selected playbook`: exact playbook id, or `none`.
- `Rejected playbooks`: similar but unsuitable playbooks and why they are not used.

Use rules:

- Use a playbook directly only when match confidence is `high`.
- For `medium`, borrow only 1-2 relevant image tasks and state the uncertainty.
- For `low`, do not use a category playbook. Continue with product facts, competitor logic, and big-brand design cases.
- If two playbooks partially match, choose the one tied to the buyer's primary purchase decision, not the product's superficial shape.
- Never force apparel or beauty "self-appearance/social attention" logic onto technical, home, tool, or appliance products.

## Output Contract

When a playbook is used, downstream briefs and layout matrices should include:

- `Category design task`
- `Buyer result`
- `Proof method`
- `Best scene`
- `Human presence guidance`
- `Misfit tropes to avoid`

## apparel_personal_style

Use when:

- The product is clothing, footwear, fashion accessories, bags, jewelry, or a wearable item bought partly for personal appearance.
- The buyer cares about fit, silhouette, styling, confidence, taste, occasion suitability, or social recognition.

Do not use when:

- The product is a technical device, appliance, fixture, or tool where the purchase decision depends mainly on performance, installation, safety, compatibility, or measurable output.

Core image tasks:

1. Self-confirmation: the buyer sees themselves looking better, more put-together, or more occasion-ready.
2. Daily integration: the item fits naturally into commute, work, casual, travel, date, party, or home routines.
3. Specific social feedback: another person notices the exact improvement, such as color, silhouette, taste, or styling.
4. Naturally noticed presence: show quiet visibility, not exaggerated envy or staged attention.
5. Styling solution: show how to pair or use the item across multiple outfits, occasions, or seasons.

Human presence guidance:

- People are usually high value.
- Use face, side face, full body, mirror moment, walking pose, detail touch, or peer interaction depending on product type.
- The product must remain the commercial hero, not become a generic fashion editorial.

Avoid:

- Overstated jealousy or "everyone staring" scenes.
- Generic model shots that do not prove fit, fabric, use occasion, or buyer result.
- Borrowing brand-logo accessories or protected fashion assets.

## projector_home_theater

Use when:

- The product is a projector, home theater display device, portable display device, or projection-related hardware.
- The purchase decision depends on picture result, screen size, setup ease, placement, connectivity, family entertainment, gaming, or room fit.

Do not use when:

- The product is only a cable, remote, tripod, screen cloth, or unrelated electronics accessory unless the image task is about projection setup support.

Core image tasks:

1. Picture-result proof: show readable subtitles, visible details, color contrast, and viewing distance, not just abstract specs.
2. Space-fit proof: show the product working in bedroom, living room, dorm, small apartment, or entertainment corner with believable scale.
3. Setup-ease proof: show placement, focus/keystone/screen-fit/stand adjustment only when confirmed for the current product; never infer automation from category or another SKU.
4. Connectivity proof: show HDMI, USB, Bluetooth, casting, game console, speaker, or laptop compatibility only when confirmed.
5. Family or personal entertainment: show movie night, bedtime viewing, kids' animation, gaming, sports, or party use without making people the main hero.
6. Structure trust: show lens, stand, ports, vents, remote, package, or control details when they help reduce purchase uncertainty.

Human presence guidance:

- People are usually medium value.
- Prefer back view, side face, hands operating controls, family sitting distance, person placing the projector, or scale reference.
- Faces can be used when they improve warmth and trust, but the projected result and product role should stay primary.

Avoid:

- Visible lens beams, projection cones, laser rays, or strong lens glow unless physically and legally appropriate.
- Outdoor cordless scenes unless built-in battery or power setup is confirmed.
- Unauthorized platform logos, app logos, Dolby/HDR/ANSI/4K/certification claims unless confirmed.
- Apparel-style "I look better / people notice me" logic.

## kitchen_appliances

Use when:

- The product is a countertop appliance, cooking tool, food prep machine, blender, coffee maker, air fryer, toaster, kettle, or related kitchen device.
- The buyer cares about output food/drink quality, speed, convenience, cleanup, health, capacity, noise, safety, or countertop fit.

Core image tasks:

1. Finished-result proof: show the food or drink outcome clearly.
2. Workflow proof: show before/use/after or step-by-step operation.
3. Capacity proof: show family portion, cup count, basket size, or ingredient volume only when accurate.
4. Cleanup proof: show removable parts, dishwasher-safe parts, nonstick surface, or wipe-clean design only when confirmed.
5. Countertop-fit proof: show realistic kitchen placement and scale.
6. Lifestyle payoff: breakfast, meal prep, family dinner, hosting, healthy snack, or office coffee routine.

Human presence guidance:

- People are usually high or medium value.
- Hands, partial body, family serving, or user operating controls are often better than full face.
- Food result should stay visually dominant when the product is outcome-driven.

Avoid:

- Unrealistic perfect food that the appliance cannot produce.
- Claims about nonstick, BPA-free, dishwasher-safe, wattage, safety certification, or health benefit without proof.
- Overcrowded countertop scenes that hide product form.

## beauty_personal_care

Use when:

- The product is skincare, hair care, grooming, makeup tool, beauty device, personal care device, or wellness-adjacent product.
- The buyer cares about appearance result, comfort, routine fit, ease of use, confidence, or before/after improvement.

Core image tasks:

1. Routine integration: show morning, evening, travel, bathroom vanity, or self-care routine.
2. Use-action proof: show application angle, contact point, grip, button, mode, or treatment area.
3. Result direction: show glow, smoothness, neatness, styling, relaxation, or cleanliness with cautious wording.
4. Detail trust: texture, applicator, brush head, material, charging, waterproof area, or hygienic storage when confirmed.
5. Social/self-confidence payoff: buyer feels more polished or cared for, but avoid medical or guaranteed result claims.

Human presence guidance:

- People are high value when safe and relevant.
- Use close-up face, hair, hand, bathroom mirror, partial body, or controlled before/after only when truthful.

Avoid:

- Medical cure claims, guaranteed before/after claims, or unrealistic skin/hair transformation.
- Using body areas or demographics not supported by the product.
- Unverified waterproof, dermatologist-tested, FDA, clinical, or hypoallergenic claims.

## home_storage_furniture

Use when:

- The product is furniture, shelf, organizer, storage, home decor utility, lighting fixture, pet/baby furniture, or room-improvement product.
- The buyer cares about room fit, capacity, style coordination, sturdiness, installation, safety, and daily organization.

Core image tasks:

1. Room transformation: show before/after organization or a calmer finished space.
2. Capacity proof: show what fits, using truthful quantity and scale.
3. Structure trust: show materials, legs, anchors, joints, load-bearing areas, drawers, hinges, or safety features when confirmed.
4. Style coordination: show how it fits modern, farmhouse, minimalist, kids room, office, or entryway decor.
5. Daily-use proof: show reaching, opening, storing, cleaning, moving, or child/pet-adjacent use when relevant and safe.

Human presence guidance:

- People are medium value.
- Use hands, partial body, family room scale, or user interaction to prove size and usability.

Avoid:

- Impossible load-bearing, unsafe child/pet scenes, or overfilled storage.
- Rooms that are too luxurious or unrelated to the product's price band.
- Decor props that make the buyer think unrelated items are included.

## outdoor_sports

Use when:

- The product is outdoor, camping, hiking, sports, fitness, travel, cycling, fishing, beach, or portable activity gear.
- The buyer cares about durability, portability, weather resistance, comfort, performance, storage, and activity fit.

Core image tasks:

1. Activity proof: show the product being used in the relevant activity, not just placed in scenery.
2. Portability proof: show carry, pack, setup, fold, storage, or transport.
3. Environment proof: show terrain, sun, rain, wind, water, trail, gym, or field only when the product supports it.
4. Scale and fit proof: show body size, backpack fit, vehicle fit, or group use.
5. Reliability proof: show materials, straps, seams, supports, grip, battery/power setup, or weather features when confirmed.

Human presence guidance:

- People are high value.
- Use action poses, hands, partial body, group use, or POV interaction to prove function.

Avoid:

- Extreme weather, water immersion, impact, or survival claims without proof.
- Unrealistic athletic performance claims.
- Unsafe scenes or incorrect protective equipment.

## tools_equipment

Use when:

- The product is a hand tool, workshop tool, repair device, measurement tool, industrial accessory, auto tool, electronic repair tool, or B2B equipment.
- The buyer cares about precision, compatibility, durability, safety, workflow speed, and job outcome.

Core image tasks:

1. Job-to-be-done proof: show the exact task the product solves.
2. Precision proof: show measurement marks, alignment, fit, grip, torque, cut, repair point, or before/after result when truthful.
3. Compatibility proof: show supported materials, sizes, models, sockets, ports, vehicles, or devices only when confirmed.
4. Durability proof: show structure, material, coating, handle, bit, case, or reinforced area.
5. Workflow proof: show step sequence, kit organization, storage case, or fast access.

Human presence guidance:

- People are medium or high value through hands and work posture.
- Prefer hands, gloves, workbench, close-up task view, or professional context over lifestyle faces.

Avoid:

- Unsafe operation, missing PPE where needed, sparks or heavy-duty claims without proof.
- Unsupported compatibility or professional certification claims.
- Decorative lifestyle scenes that do not prove the work result.
